# Interview questions: the thin-evidence path

Fire this path only when the source samples are too thin to extract a reliable profile, or when the subject is the operator themselves and you can ask directly. The goal is to gather enough concrete material that the extraction rubric has something real to work with. Always prefer real samples over self-report: people describe their voice less accurately than their writing reveals it.

## How to run it

Every question goes through AskUserQuestion. Ask in small batches, not all at once. Attach a recommended or example answer to each so the subject reacts rather than starting cold. After the interview, treat the answers as a sample to analyze, not as the profile itself. The subject's own phrasing in their answers is itself evidence; mine it.

## Question bank

### Identity and use
- Whose voice is this, and what will the profile be used to write?
- Which contexts matter most (email, posts, prose, dialogue, marketing)?

### Calibration by reaction
- Paste two short pieces of writing, one that sounds exactly like the subject and one that does not. What is wrong with the second one? (The contrast is high-signal.)
- Name a writer or voice the subject is often compared to, and one they are never like.

### Mechanics
- Long sentences or short? Formal or plain? Do they use contractions? Em-dashes? Emoji?
- How do they open a message? How do they sign off?
- What words or phrases do they reach for constantly? What words make them cringe?

### Negative space
- What does the subject never do in writing, even when it would be easy?
- What is the fastest way to sound fake as the subject?

### Stance
- How do they deliver bad news or disagree?
- How much do they hedge versus assert?

## After the interview

Run the answers through the same extraction rubric, citing the subject's own answer text as evidence. Mark the profile's confidence as interview-derived, which is weaker than corpus-derived, and recommend collecting real samples to firm it up.
