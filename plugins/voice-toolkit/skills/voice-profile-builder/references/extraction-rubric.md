# Extraction rubric: the dimensions to analyze

Work through every dimension below against the cleaned corpus. For each trait you observe, pull at least one verbatim quote as evidence. The evidence rule is absolute: no quote, no trait. A described trait with no quote behind it is a guess, and guesses produce confident wrong output.

## The evidence rule

Each trait entry has two parts:

- Trait: a one-line statement of the habit.
- Evidence: one or more verbatim quotes from the corpus that prove it.

If you cannot find a quote, you cannot list the trait. If you find one weak example, mark the trait low-confidence. Frequency matters: a habit seen once is a maybe, a habit seen across many samples is a rule.

## Dimensions

1. **Diction and vocabulary.** Plain vs ornate, Latinate vs Anglo-Saxon, technical vs lay, profane vs clean, archaic vs current. Note signature words that recur.
2. **Sentence length and rhythm.** Average length. Does the subject run long and periodic, or short and clipped? Do they vary deliberately for effect? Quote a long one and a short one.
3. **Syntax habits.** Fragments. Inversions. Front-loaded subordinate clauses. Lists and triads. Parallelism. Run-ons by design.
4. **Punctuation tics.** Em-dashes, semicolons, colons, ellipses, parentheticals. Caps, italics. How they handle pauses. Record what the subject actually does, never what a style guide prefers.
5. **Openings and closings.** How sentences, paragraphs, and whole pieces begin and end. Greetings and sign-offs for correspondence. First moves in dialogue.
6. **Structure and flow.** How the subject orders an argument or a scene. Do they front the point or build to it? Tangents and asides? Returns to a motif?
7. **Directness and stance.** Blunt vs hedged. Assertive vs qualified. How they handle disagreement, bad news, a hard ask.
8. **Warmth and emotional register.** Cool and detached, or warm and personal? How affection, anger, grief, or humor surface in the text.
9. **Figurative language.** Metaphors, analogies, imagery. Are they specific and earthy, or abstract? Recurring images? Do they stack metaphors or use one clean one?
10. **What the subject avoids.** The negative space. Constructions, words, moves, and tones that never appear. This feeds anti-rules.
11. **Register shifts and their frequency.** How the voice changes across contexts (formal vs casual, calm vs heated, public vs private). Each distinct register may need its own file. Also estimate how often each register actually appears: the loud, quotable register is usually the rarest. Record the plain-to-ornate ratio (roughly, the share of short/plain lines vs set-piece flourishes) so the profile can lead with the frequent baseline instead of the memorable exception.
12. **Tells and quirks.** The idiosyncratic moves that make the voice unmistakable. The thing you could not mistake for anyone else.

## Multi-speaker sources

If the corpus has many speakers (a transcript, a group thread), you must first isolate the subject's lines before analyzing. Profiling the blend of everyone produces a voice that belongs to no one. Confirm whose voice in Step 1 and filter to it.

## Confidence

Close the extraction with a one-line confidence read: which dimensions are well-evidenced, which rest on one or two examples, and which registers are missing from the corpus entirely. Carry this into the profile's README.
