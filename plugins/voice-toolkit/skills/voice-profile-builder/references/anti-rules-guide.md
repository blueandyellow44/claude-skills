# Anti-rules guide: writing constraints that actually work

Anti-rules are the negative space of a voice. They tell a drafter what never to do. They matter more than positive traits, because a model can hit most positive traits and still break the voice with one wrong move (a contraction the subject never uses, a hedge they never reach for, an emoji they would never send). Anti-rules catch those.

## Why anti-rules outperform positive rules

Positive rules describe a target. Anti-rules fence off the ways to miss it. A drafter can read "warm and direct" and still produce something off. An anti-rule like "never opens with 'I hope this finds you well'" is checkable, binary, and impossible to misread. The best profiles are heavy on anti-rules.

## Anatomy of an anti-rule

Each anti-rule has three parts. Lead with the rule, then explain, then say when it applies.

```
Rule: Never stack two metaphors in one passage.
Why: The subject uses one clean image and trusts it. Stacked metaphors read as trying too hard, the opposite of the subject's confidence.
How to apply: If a draft has a second figurative image within a few sentences of the first, cut the weaker one.
```

The Why is not decoration. It lets a drafter judge edge cases instead of following the rule blindly. The How-to-apply turns the rule into an action during editing.

## Where anti-rules come from

- Dimension 10 of the extraction rubric (what the subject avoids).
- The contrast between the subject and a generic AI default. If the AI default is to hedge and the subject never hedges, that is an anti-rule.
- Corrections during the blind-draft test. Every time a test draft sounds wrong, the fix becomes an anti-rule.

## Grounding

Anti-rules still need evidence, but the evidence is an absence plus a contrast. Support each one by showing the subject consistently doing the opposite, or by noting that the construction appears nowhere in a substantial corpus. Do not invent an anti-rule from a stereotype of the subject; derive it from the text.

## Common high-value anti-rules to check for

- Banned openings and sign-offs (the greeting or closing the subject would never use).
- Banned words and phrases (corporate filler, AI tells, register-breaking slang or formality).
- Punctuation the subject never uses.
- Hedging or qualifying the subject does not do.
- Sentimentality, self-promotion, or announcer lines the subject avoids.
- Formatting the subject would never reach for (bullet lists in prose, all-caps, emoji).

A profile with ten well-grounded anti-rules constrains output far better than one with twenty vague positive adjectives.
